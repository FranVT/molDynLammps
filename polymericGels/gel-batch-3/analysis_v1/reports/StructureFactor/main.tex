\justifying

\paragraph{Characteristic distances in the system.}
The distance range for the pair-wise interaction is \(r_{b}\in[0.4,0.6]\).
The total distance between the center of two central particles that aire in a pairwise interaction is \(r_{\mathrm{int}}=0.8+r_b\therefore r_{\mathrm{int}}\in[1.2,1.4]\).
By doind some math we get that if we increase the amount of central particles in a line within the pairwise distance interaction range, the distance increases as follows, \(d_{\mathrm{int}}(N_{\mathrm{central}}) = \left(N_{\mathrm{central}}-1\right)\left(2\sigma_p + r_b\right)\).

\paragraph{Domains for the fits.} 
From the characteristic distances we can define the following spatial domains \(\lambda_1\in\left[ 7/2\sigma_p,21/2\sigma_p \right]\) and \(\lambda_2\in\left[ 21/2\sigma_p,L/4 \right]\).

\paragraph{About the fit.}
The experimental data was subjected to a fit
\begin{gather*}
    f(\abs{\vec{q}}) = \frac{p_1}{\abs{\vec{q}}^{p_2}}.
\end{gather*}
